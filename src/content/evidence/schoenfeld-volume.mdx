---
source: schoenfeld-volume
claim: Muscle growth from resistance training follows a graded dose-response with weekly set volume; more weekly sets per muscle group produce larger hypertrophy within the range studied.
design: Systematic review and meta-regression of 15 resistance-training studies comparing weekly set volumes per muscle group with hypertrophy measured by imaging or thickness.
population: Healthy adults, mostly untrained or recreationally trained, in supervised resistance-training studies of 6 to 24 weeks.
effect: Each additional weekly set was associated with about a 0.37 percent greater increase in muscle size. Ten or more weekly sets per muscle group produced roughly 9.8 percent growth versus about 5.4 percent for fewer than five sets.
certainty: low
caveats: Small number of studies, mostly untrained participants and short durations, so the upper limit of the dose-response is not defined. The outcome is muscle size, not strength or pain. Volume tolerance varies with recovery, training age and other stressors, so the trend informs planning rather than setting a target.
