---
source: owen-2020-bjsm-nma
claim: Several exercise modes help chronic low back pain. Resistance training, stabilisation/motor-control exercise, Pilates and aerobic exercise each outperformed control for pain and function, with no single mode clearly superior.
design: Network meta-analysis of 89 randomised controlled trials (5578 participants) comparing exercise modes for chronic non-specific low back pain; outcomes pain, physical function and mental health.
population: Adults with chronic (over 12 weeks) non-specific low back pain, mostly recruited from community and outpatient settings.
effect: Pilates, stabilisation/motor-control, resistance and aerobic training were all effective versus true control for pain and function; rankings between modes overlapped widely. Resistance and stabilisation also showed gains in mental health. Stretching and McKenzie-style approaches ranked lower but confidence intervals were wide.
certainty: low
caveats: The authors rate the overall quality of evidence as low. Many trials were small, short and at high risk of bias, and the rankings are sensitive to which trials are included. The result supports choosing a mode the person will actually do, not a hierarchy of modes.
