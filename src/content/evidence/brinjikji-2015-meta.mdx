---
source: brinjikji-2015-meta
claim: Some MRI findings of disc degeneration, in particular disc bulge, protrusion, extrusion and Modic 1 change, are more prevalent in adults with low back pain than in asymptomatic controls, especially under age 50.
design: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 case-control studies comparing MRI findings between adults with low back pain and asymptomatic controls (3097 individuals in total).
population: Adults aged 50 or younger in most analyses, comparing symptomatic low back pain cases with asymptomatic controls from the same study populations.
effect: Disc bulge (odds ratio about 7.5), spondylolysis (about 5), extrusion (about 4.4), protrusion (about 2.6) and Modic 1 change (about 4) were all significantly more prevalent in people with low back pain under 50. Disc degeneration itself was also more prevalent in symptomatic adults (odds ratio about 2.2).
certainty: moderate
caveats: Association at group level is not causation for an individual, and the companion asymptomatic-prevalence review (brinjikji-2015-degeneration) shows the same findings are common without pain. Taken together the two papers mean an imaging finding raises or lowers probability rather than settling the question. Included studies were heterogeneous and many had small samples.
