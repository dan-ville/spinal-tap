---
source: deadlift-loads-2022
claim: Modelled lumbar spinal loads during heavy deadlifting are very high, with compression estimated in the range of 5 to 18 kN and anterior shear 1.3 to 3.2 kN, and the lumbar spine tolerates them in trained lifters.
design: Narrative review (2022) of biomechanical studies estimating lumbar compression and shear during the deadlift using inverse-dynamics and musculoskeletal models.
population: Trained and competitive lifters measured in laboratory deadlift studies; modelled loads rather than directly measured in-vivo forces.
effect: Reported peak L4-L5 or L5-S1 compression spanned roughly 5 to 18 kN and anterior shear roughly 1.3 to 3.2 kN depending on load, technique, model and lifter size. Loads scale with barbell load and with the horizontal distance between bar and spine.
certainty: low
topic: biomechanics
kind: narrative
caveats: Values are model outputs with large between-study variation and depend heavily on modelling assumptions. No biomechanics studies of repetitive deadlifting exist, so nothing here describes how loads accumulate across sets, sessions or a training block. Cadaveric failure thresholds (adams-dolan) should not be compared directly with these numbers because living trained tissue adapts.
