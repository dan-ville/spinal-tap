---
source: louw-2016-pne
claim: Pain neuroscience education improves pain, disability, pain catastrophising and fear of movement in people with chronic musculoskeletal pain, with the clearest effects when combined with movement or exercise.
design: Systematic review of 13 randomised controlled trials of pain neuroscience education for musculoskeletal pain, with narrative synthesis.
population: Adults with chronic musculoskeletal pain, mostly chronic low back pain, in outpatient and physiotherapy settings.
effect: Across trials, education about pain biology produced improvements in pain ratings, disability, catastrophising, fear-avoidance beliefs, pain knowledge and health-care use. Effects on pain intensity alone were small; the larger changes were in beliefs and function, especially when education accompanied active treatment.
certainty: low
caveats: Trials were small and heterogeneous, with varied education content and delivery, and a narrative synthesis rather than a pooled estimate. Education alone is a modest intervention; the review supports pairing it with graded activity rather than treating it as a standalone fix. Later meta-analyses give similar small to moderate estimates.
