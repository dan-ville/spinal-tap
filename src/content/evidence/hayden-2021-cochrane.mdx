---
source: hayden-2021-cochrane
claim: Exercise therapy probably reduces pain and improves function in chronic low back pain compared with no treatment, usual care or placebo, with small to moderate effects.
design: Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis of 249 randomised controlled trials (24486 participants) of exercise therapy for chronic low back pain.
population: Adults with chronic (over 12 weeks) non-specific low back pain, across clinical and community settings worldwide.
effect: Versus no treatment, usual care or placebo, exercise reduced pain by about 15 points on a 0 to 100 scale and improved function by about 7 points at earliest follow-up; effects persisted at long-term follow-up but were smaller. Exercise was also modestly better than other conservative treatments.
certainty: moderate
caveats: Effects are small to moderate at group level and many trials were at risk of bias and small. The review does not identify a superior exercise type; population and dose varied widely. Clinically important thresholds are debated, so a moderate effect for a group can mean a large effect for some individuals and none for others.
