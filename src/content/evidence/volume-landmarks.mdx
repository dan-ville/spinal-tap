source: volume-landmarks
claim: The volume-landmark model (MV, MEV, MAV, MRV) is a programming heuristic that frames weekly set counts per muscle group as a range from maintenance through most-productive to the maximum recoverable, with progression across a block and a deload once recovery lags.
design: Coaching model published as practitioner articles; it synthesises training principles and dose-response literature into named thresholds. It is a model for planning, not a measurement from a trial.
population: Resistance-trained adults planning hypertrophy or strength blocks; the suggested numbers are generic and not derived from any clinical population.
effect: No measured effect. The model proposes starting a block near the minimum effective volume, adding sets week to week while recovery allows, and deloading when the maximum recoverable volume is reached. Its usefulness is as a shared vocabulary for dosing and for noticing when accumulated fatigue is driving symptoms.
certainty: very-low
topic: training
kind: narrative
caveats: Marked as a model, not a measurement. Specific set ranges are coach estimates and have not been validated in trials, and individual landmarks shift with sleep, stress, training age and pain. Use it to structure a block and interpret the log, never as a target that overrides symptoms.
