source: resorption-2025-review
claim: Spontaneous regression of lumbar disc herniation is the usual course under conservative care, typically visible on imaging within about six months.
design: Systematic review (2025) of studies reporting repeat imaging in conservatively managed lumbar disc herniation, updating earlier meta-analyses.
population: Adults with symptomatic lumbar disc herniation managed without surgery who underwent follow-up MRI or CT.
effect: About 77% of herniations showed regression on follow-up imaging, most within roughly six months. Larger, extruded and sequestered herniations regressed most often and most completely, consistent with the earlier meta-analysis (zhong-2017-resorption).
certainty: moderate
topic: imaging
kind: systematic-review
caveats: Study designs and regression definitions vary and the pooled percentage should be read as an approximate range rather than a precise rate. Regression on imaging is not the same as recovery, and recovery often precedes it; the finding supports patience with conservative care, not a guarantee of outcome for any one person. Licence and full-text access were confirmed through PMC at the time of registration; check the record if the figures are quoted exactly.
