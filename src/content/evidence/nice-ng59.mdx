---
source: nice-ng59
claim: NICE NG59 (2016, updated 2020) recommends self-management advice and group exercise for low back pain with or without sciatica, considering manual therapy or psychological therapy only as part of a package that includes exercise, and advises against routine imaging, belts, traction, acupuncture and most drug classes.
design: National clinical guideline (United Kingdom) built from systematic reviews and network meta-analyses graded with GRADE, with health-economic modelling; published 2016 with drug recommendations amended in 2020.
population: People aged 16 and over with non-specific low back pain with or without sciatica, in any NHS setting.
effect: Group exercise (biomechanical, aerobic, mind-body or a mix) is the first-line active treatment. Manual therapy and psychological (CBT-based) therapy are to be offered only within a multimodal package that includes exercise. Imaging is not to be offered routinely in non-specialist settings. Paracetamol alone, opioids for chronic LBP, SSRIs/SNRIs/TCAs/gabapentinoids for LBP, belts, corsets, foot orthotics, traction, acupuncture, TENS and ultrasound are recommended against.
certainty: moderate
caveats: The July 2026 surveillance update reportedly removed the psychological-therapy recommendations; this is unverified unless confirmed against nice.org.uk, so treat the psychological-therapy line as provisional. Many recommendations rest on low to moderate certainty evidence and on UK cost-effectiveness analyses that may not transfer to other systems. NICE does not specify exercise dose.
