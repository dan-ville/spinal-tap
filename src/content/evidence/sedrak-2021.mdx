---
source: sedrak-2021
claim: In elite athletes with symptomatic lumbar disc herniation, return-to-play rates after non-operative and operative treatment are similar.
design: Systematic review and meta-analysis of studies reporting return to play in elite athletes with symptomatic lumbar disc herniation, comparing operative with non-operative management.
population: Elite and professional athletes across sports with imaging-confirmed symptomatic lumbar disc herniation.
effect: About 82% of athletes treated non-operatively and about 83% treated operatively returned to play, with no statistically significant difference between groups. Time to return was in the range of about four to five months in both arms where reported.
certainty: low
topic: return-to-sport
kind: systematic-review
caveats: The evidence is observational, with treatment allocation by clinical judgement rather than randomisation, so sicker or more refractory cases may have been more likely to have surgery. Elite athletes have access to resources most people do not. Return to play is not the same as symptom resolution or career longevity. Overley et al. 2016 (overley-2016) reports consistent microdiscectomy return figures.
