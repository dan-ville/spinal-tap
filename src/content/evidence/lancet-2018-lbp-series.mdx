source: lancet-2018-lbp-series
claim: Low back pain is the leading cause of disability worldwide, most episodes are non-specific, and guideline-discordant care (imaging, opioids, surgery, rest) is widespread while recommended active care (education, exercise, staying active at work) is underused.
design: Three-paper narrative series in The Lancet by an international author group, synthesising Global Burden of Disease data, epidemiology, guideline comparison and health-system evidence; not a systematic review.
population: Global adult population with low back pain across high-, middle- and low-income countries; guideline comparison across the United States, United Kingdom and Denmark.
effect: Lifetime prevalence is high and years lived with disability from LBP rose by over 50% between 1990 and 2015. Guidelines consistently recommend education, reassurance, activity and exercise as first-line care; passive, pharmacological and surgical care are overused. The series proposes a call for action to realign care with evidence.
certainty: moderate
topic: treatment
kind: narrative
caveats: A narrative series and position piece, not a quantitative synthesis; its estimates of burden and of care discordance draw on heterogeneous data and expert consensus. It gives no exercise dose or programme guidance. The full text is free only with registration.
