---
source: steele-2014-lumbar-extension
claim: People with chronic low back pain often show specific deconditioning of the lumbar extensor muscles (reduced strength, endurance and cross-sectional area), and isolated lumbar extension training can reverse these changes alongside symptom improvement.
design: Narrative review pooling three lines of evidence (cross-sectional comparisons, imaging and histology, and training interventions) on lumbar extensor deconditioning in low back pain.
population: Adults with chronic low back pain compared with asymptomatic controls, plus training studies in symptomatic adults.
effect: Lumbar extensor strength, endurance and multifidus size are commonly reduced in chronic LBP; isolated lumbar extension training with pelvic stabilisation increases extensor strength and is associated with reductions in pain and disability in the trials reviewed.
certainty: low
topic: treatment
kind: narrative
caveats: Narrative rather than systematic review, and the authors have a research focus on isolated lumbar extension machines. Whether deconditioning is cause or consequence of pain is not resolved. Improvements in pain do not always track improvements in strength, so the mechanism may not be purely muscular.
