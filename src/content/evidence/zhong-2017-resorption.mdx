---
source: zhong-2017-resorption
claim: A majority of lumbar disc herniations show spontaneous resorption on follow-up imaging without surgery.
design: Meta-analysis of 11 cohort studies (360 patients) reporting repeat imaging of lumbar disc herniations managed conservatively.
population: Adults with imaging-confirmed lumbar disc herniation treated without surgery and re-imaged during follow-up.
effect: The pooled incidence of spontaneous resorption was about 67% (95% CI roughly 51% to 80%). Sequestered and extruded herniations resorbed more often than contained protrusions. Follow-up intervals were typically in the range of several months to about a year.
certainty: moderate
topic: imaging
kind: systematic-review
caveats: Included cohorts were small and heterogeneous in how resorption was defined and measured, and the pooled estimate has a wide confidence interval. Imaging resorption does not map one-to-one onto symptom change; people improve with and without visible regression. The later 2025 review (resorption-2025-review) reports a higher pooled figure.
