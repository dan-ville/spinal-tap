---
source: helms-rpe-rir
claim: A repetitions-in-reserve based RPE scale lets lifters regulate load set by set; it tracks bar velocity and proximity to failure reasonably well in trained lifters and can replace or complement percentage-based loading.
design: Narrative review and practical framework describing the RIR-based RPE scale, summarising validation data and proposing how to use it for autoregulated resistance training.
population: Resistance-trained adults, particularly powerlifters and strength athletes; the validation data come mainly from trained lifters.
effect: Ratings correlate with average concentric velocity and with actual repetitions left in reserve, with better accuracy closer to failure and in more experienced lifters. The scale allows daily adjustment of load to readiness without requiring a 1RM test.
certainty: low
topic: training
kind: narrative
caveats: Accuracy falls at low intensities, far from failure, and in novices, who tend to under-rate effort. The framework itself is expert opinion built on small validation studies. It is a tool for choosing load on the day, not a measure of tissue tolerance or safety.
