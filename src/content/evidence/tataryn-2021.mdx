---
source: tataryn-2021
claim: Posterior-chain resistance training (hip hinge, deadlift and back-extension variants) reduced pain and disability in chronic low back pain at least as much as general exercise or walking programmes.
design: Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials comparing posterior-chain resistance training with general exercise or walking for chronic low back pain.
population: Adults with chronic low back pain from the general population; trials excluded specific pathologies such as fracture, infection or tumour.
effect: Posterior-chain resistance training produced greater improvements in pain, disability and muscular strength than general exercise or walking across the pooled trials; the between-group differences were modest and the number of trials was small.
certainty: low
caveats: Few trials (eight), small samples and heterogeneous programmes. Follow-up was mostly short. The finding is that loaded hip-hinge and extension training is a reasonable option, not that it is required; general exercise and walking also improved outcomes.
