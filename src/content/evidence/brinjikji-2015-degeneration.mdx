---
source: brinjikji-2015-degeneration
claim: Imaging findings of spinal degeneration are common in people with no back pain and become more common with age, so a degenerative finding on its own does not explain symptoms.
design: Systematic review of 33 studies reporting CT or MRI findings in 3110 asymptomatic individuals, with prevalence estimated by decade of age.
population: Asymptomatic adults aged 20 to 80 across the included imaging studies; no participant had back pain at the time of imaging.
effect: Disc degeneration was present in about 37% of asymptomatic 20-year-olds rising to about 96% of 80-year-olds. Disc bulge rose from about 30% to 84%, protrusion from 29% to 43%, and annular fissure from 19% to 29% across the same age span.
certainty: moderate
topic: imaging
kind: systematic-review
caveats: These are prevalence figures in pain-free people, not proof that imaging findings are irrelevant. The companion meta-analysis (brinjikji-2015-meta) shows some of the same findings are more common in people with pain, so read the two cards together. Studies varied in imaging technique and reporting definitions, and prevalence was pooled across heterogeneous populations.
