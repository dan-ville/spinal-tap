---
source: adams-dolan
claim: In cadaveric and in-vitro work, disc prolapse is produced by compression combined with flexion or repeated flexion, whereas pure compression in a neutral posture tends to fail the vertebral endplate before the disc.
design: Narrative review of spine biomechanics drawing on the authors' cadaveric loading experiments and other in-vitro studies of disc failure mechanisms.
population: Cadaveric human lumbar motion segments loaded in laboratory rigs; no living participants.
effect: Severe or repeated flexion under compressive load drove nucleus material posteriorly through the annulus in a proportion of specimens, particularly younger discs with a fluid nucleus. Neutral compression most often caused endplate fracture rather than prolapse. Sustained loading and time of day altered disc height and mechanics.
certainty: low
caveats: Cadaveric tissue without muscle, blood supply or adaptation cannot show what happens in a living, trained spine over months of loading. Failure loads and postures in the lab do not translate directly into safe or unsafe lifting rules; the human in-vivo literature does not show that lumbar flexion during lifting predicts injury. Treat as mechanism, not prescription.
