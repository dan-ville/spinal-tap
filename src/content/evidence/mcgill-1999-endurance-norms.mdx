---
source: mcgill-1999-endurance-norms
claim: Trunk endurance hold times for flexion, extension and side bridge in healthy young adults provide reference values and ratios that can be used as training and testing targets.
design: Normative database study measuring isometric hold times for trunk flexor, Biering-Sorensen extensor and side bridge tests in healthy university-aged adults, with test-retest reliability.
population: 75 healthy young adults (mean age about 21) without back pain; not a clinical or athletic population.
effect: Mean hold times were roughly 134 s (men) and 185 s (women) for extension, 136 s and 134 s for flexion, and 94 to 99 s (men) and 72 to 78 s (women) for side bridge. Suggested ratios for a balanced profile were flexion to extension below 1.0, right-to-left side bridge difference under about 5%, and side bridge to extension around 0.75. Reliability across days was high.
certainty: low
caveats: Norms come from a small sample of healthy students and the ratios are proposed clinical targets, not validated predictors of injury or outcome. Later work has found weak and inconsistent links between endurance scores and back pain. Use the numbers as a repeatable baseline to retest against, not as a threshold that proves readiness.
