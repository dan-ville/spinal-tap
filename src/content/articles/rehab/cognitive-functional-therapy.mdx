<Lede>Cognitive functional therapy is a clinician-led approach that combines making sense of the pain, graded re-exposure to feared movements and lifestyle change. The RESTORE trial found it produced large reductions in activity limitation that held at a year, and that adding movement sensors added nothing. It is the strongest single trial in this track, and it is a trial of a trained clinician's programme, not of a self-directed one.</Lede>

## What cognitive functional therapy is

Cognitive functional therapy, usually shortened to CFT, is a physiotherapy-led approach built from three components. The first is making sense of the pain: a structured conversation in which the person's own story, beliefs and fears are drawn out and reframed against what is known about pain and tissue. The second is exposure with control: the feared or avoided movements are performed, graded, with attention to relaxing the guarding and bracing that have grown around them. The third is lifestyle change: sleep, activity levels, stress and the habits that keep a sensitized system sensitized.

If that sounds like the [pain-science track](/learn/pain-science/) turned into a clinical programme, it is. The reframing component draws on the same education literature; pain neuroscience education on its own produces changes in beliefs and fear with modest effects on pain and disability <Source id="louw-2016-pne" />. The exposure component is [graded exposure](/learn/pain-science/graded-exposure/) delivered by a clinician who can watch the guarding and coach it out. CFT's claim is that combining those pieces, individualised to the person's own story, does more than either does alone.

## What RESTORE tested

RESTORE was a three-arm randomised trial run in Australia. Four hundred and ninety-two adults with low back pain lasting over three months and at least moderate activity limitation were randomised to CFT, CFT plus movement-sensor biofeedback, or usual care. The primary outcome was activity limitation on the Roland Morris Disability Questionnaire, followed for fifty-two weeks <Source id="kent-2023-restore" />.

<Evidence id="kent-2023-restore" />

Both CFT arms reduced activity limitation by about four and a half to five points more than usual care at thirteen weeks, and the difference held at a year. That is a large effect for a chronic-pain trial; most interventions in this field produce differences of a point or two that fade. The biofeedback sensors added nothing, which is a useful finding in its own right. The technology was not the active ingredient. The clinician and the programme were.

The card rates certainty high, and it earns that. The trial was large, pre-registered, properly randomised and followed up for a year with low loss. It is the best single piece of evidence in this track.

## What RESTORE did not test

Three things, and each matters for a reader of this site.

**It did not test self-directed CFT.** The clinicians in RESTORE were trained for over a hundred hours and supervised throughout. The programme was individualised by a professional who could see the person move, hear their story and adjust in real time. Nothing in the trial says that reading about CFT and applying it to yourself produces the same effect, and the authors themselves note that routine-practice effects are likely to be smaller than trial effects. A self-coaching lifter can borrow the ideas. They cannot borrow the trial result.

**It did not test CFT against another active treatment.** The comparator was usual care, which was heterogeneous and which participants knew they had been assigned to. The [exercise modes article](/learn/rehab/exercise-modes-compared/) shows several exercise modes beating control; RESTORE shows CFT beating a mixed bag of ordinary care. Whether CFT beats a well-run exercise programme delivered with the same contact time is a different trial, and it has not been run at this scale.

**It did not test a population of trained lifters.** Participants had chronic, disabling pain and were recruited from the community. Many had been avoiding movement for years. The size of the effect may reflect how much ground there was to recover; someone who is already lifting and already moving has less of that ground.

## Where it sits in the guidelines

The WHO guideline, published the same year, lists cognitive behavioural therapy among its conditional recommendations for chronic primary low back pain; CFT is not CBT, but it shares the premise that beliefs and behaviour around pain are treatable targets <Source id="who-2023-clbp" />. NICE NG59 makes psychological therapy available only as part of a package that includes exercise, which is structurally what CFT is, though the [guidelines article](/learn/rehab/what-the-guidelines-say/) notes that line now carries an unverified surveillance caveat <Source id="nice-ng59" />.

RESTORE was published after both guidelines were drafted. A future revision may weight it more heavily. For now, CFT sits as the best-evidenced single programme in a field whose guidelines still speak in categories.

## What a self-coaching lifter can take from it

The transferable content is the structure, not the result.

**Make sense of the pain first.** Before loading anything, have a working explanation for why it hurts that does not depend on a tissue being broken. The [pain vs damage](/learn/pain-science/pain-vs-damage/) and [sensitization](/learn/pain-science/sensitization/) articles supply that; so does the broader education literature the reframing component draws on <Source id="moseley-butler-explain-pain" />.

**Expose with control, not with bracing.** The CFT exposure component pays as much attention to how a movement is performed as to whether it is. Guarded, breath-held, over-braced movement is treated as part of the problem. For a lifter that means the exposure session is not the place for a maximal brace and a grind; it is the place for calm, relaxed, completable reps. The [graded exposure article](/learn/pain-science/graded-exposure/) sets the dials.

**Treat lifestyle as training.** Sleep, stress and activity outside the gym were explicit targets in RESTORE. The [self-coaching track](/learn/self-coaching/) builds them into the log, because preparedness on the day decides how far the other dials move.

**Know when you need the clinician.** The trial result belongs to supervised, individualised care. If self-direction is not moving the needle after a fair block, the evidence-based next step is a clinician trained in this kind of programme, not a harder self-directed one. Any of the red flags at the top of this page is a reason to go sooner.

## Key points

<KeyList>

1. **CFT has three parts.** Making sense of the pain, graded exposure with control over guarding, and lifestyle change, individualised by a clinician.
2. **RESTORE is the strongest trial in this track.** Large, pre-registered, year-long, high certainty: CFT reduced activity limitation by about four and a half to five points more than usual care, and the effect held.
3. **Sensors added nothing.** The clinician and the programme were the active ingredient, not the technology.
4. **It tested supervised care, against usual care, in a disabled community sample.** It did not test self-direction, did not compare with a matched exercise programme, and did not study trained lifters.
5. **Borrow the structure, not the result.** Explain the pain, expose calmly, treat lifestyle as training, and route to a clinician when self-direction stalls.
6. **Red flags leave the scope.** RESTORE excluded specific causes; so does every article in this track.

</KeyList>

<Check id="cognitive-functional-therapy-1" kind="concept">
In your own words, explain why a lifter cannot claim the RESTORE trial result as evidence that applying CFT principles to their own training will work as well.
<Fragment slot="answer">
RESTORE tested a programme delivered by clinicians with over a hundred hours of training and ongoing supervision, individualised in real time to each person, in a community sample with chronic disabling pain and years of avoidance to recover from. A self-directed version removes the clinician, the supervision and the individualisation, and applies the ideas to a different population, so the trial's effect size does not transfer even if the principles do. Why it matters: the structure of CFT is worth borrowing, but the honest expectation for self-direction is a smaller effect and a lower threshold for seeking a trained clinician.
</Fragment>

<Check id="cognitive-functional-therapy-2" kind="recall">
What did the biofeedback arm of RESTORE show, and why is that finding useful?
<Fragment slot="answer">
Adding movement-sensor biofeedback to CFT produced the same reduction in activity limitation as CFT alone, so the sensors added nothing. It is useful because it locates the active ingredient in the clinician-led programme rather than in a technology, which argues against spending on wearables or apps as a substitute for the reframing and exposure work. Why it matters: it is a rare clean test of a technology add-on, and it came back negative.
</Fragment>
