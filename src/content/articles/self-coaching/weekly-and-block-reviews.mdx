<Lede>The daily tree decides a session; it cannot raise a budget, change a phase or notice a drift. Those decisions belong to two fixed reviews, weekly and at the end of each block, each with a short list of questions answered from the log. This article gives both agendas, works one of each from a real-looking log, and explains why the reviews are the only place budgets and phases move.</Lede>

## Why reviews are separate from the tree

The [daily decision tree](/learn/self-coaching/daily-decision-tree/) runs on one morning's reading and can only make a session smaller or hold it. It is deliberately not allowed to make the week larger, because a lifter who feels good on Thursday is the least reliable judge of what Thursday should contain. The [spike guard](/learn/training-science/workload-spikes-and-acwr/) says the budget is "raised only at a block review, never mid-week"; this article is where that happens.

A review is a fixed appointment with a fixed agenda. It is held at the same time each week whether or not anything went wrong, and the agenda is answered from the [log](/learn/self-coaching/the-log/), not from how the week felt.

## The weekly review

Held once a week, after the last session and before the next week's first. Seven questions.

| # | Question | Where it is read |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How many bad verdicts, and on which movements? | Daily verdicts |
| 2 | Did any movement regress, and is it still regressed? | Tree decisions |
| 3 | Was the axial hard-set budget met, under, or cut? | Training-day sets at RPE ≥ 7 |
| 4 | Did any session exceed the trailing-30-day maximum? | Load and sets per lift |
| 5 | What were the week's sleep and stress averages, and any outliers? | Daily fields |
| 6 | Is bodyweight trending? | Daily field |
| 7 | Any below-glute yes? If so, was the red-flag list read that day? | Daily field |

What the weekly review may do:

- **Restore a cut budget.** A week cut by 40 percent after two bad days reopens at its full value the following week if that week ended with no further bad verdict. If it did not, the cut carries one more week.
- **Un-regress a movement.** A movement that regressed and has since had two clean verdicts following its sessions climbs one rung back. One rung, in reverse order: load, then moment arm, then range.
- **Flag a drift.** Three yellow verdicts on the same movement in two weeks, with no bad, is a drift. The tree never caught it because yellow holds. The review marks it; the next session on that movement is treated as a regression of one rung.
- **Note a preparedness pattern.** A sleep average under a chosen floor or a stress outlier week is recorded as context for the block review.

What the weekly review may not do: raise the budget above its block value, add a lift, change a phase. Those are the block review's.

## The block review

Held at the end of each block, which in the [plan](/plan/) means every 4 to 6 weeks in Phase 2 and beyond, coinciding with the [deload](/learn/training-science/deloads/) <Source id="bell-2023-deload-delphi" />. Six questions.

| # | Question | Where it is read |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clean weeks in a row? | Weekly reviews |
| 2 | Which phase gates are met, by the log's numbers? | The [plan](/plan/) gates |
| 3 | Did the monthly McGill retest hold the ratios? | Battery times <Source id="mcgill-1999-endurance-norms" /> |
| 4 | Did the block's volume produce the expected progression, or stall? | Load and e1RM trend per lift |
| 5 | Did any movement spend the block regressed? | Weekly reviews |
| 6 | Was the budget cut more than once this block? | Weekly reviews |

What the block review may do:

- **Raise the budget**, by a step the lifter sets in advance, typically one or two hard sets a week, if the block had no cut and the progression held. The dose-response relationship between weekly sets and strength is the reason to raise it at all, and its diminishing returns are the reason to raise it slowly <Source id="schoenfeld-volume" />.
- **Hold the budget** if the block had one cut or a stall. A stall with no cut is a signal to look at the [progression model](/learn/training-science/progression-models/), not at the volume.
- **Lower the budget** if the block had two or more cuts. The budget was above tolerance; the block proves it.
- **Advance the phase** if every gate on the plan reads met from the log, not from memory.
- **Set the next block's structure.** In Phase 3 that is which block of the [periodization](/learn/training-science/periodization/) sequence comes next; the evidence favours varying the structure over not varying it, and is indifferent among the ways of doing so <Source id="williams-2017-periodization-meta" />.

## A weekly review worked

Lena, Phase 1, budget 8 hard sets, two sessions, baseline stiffness 2. Her week:

| Day | Verdict on prior session | Sets at RPE ≥ 7 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | clean | 4 | RDL 70 kg 3×10, trap bar 90 kg |
| Tue | yellow | | stiffness 4, settled |
| Wed | | | walk only |
| Thu | | 4 | RDL 70 kg 3×10, trap bar 90 kg |
| Fri | yellow | | stiffness 4, settled |
| Sat–Sun | | | |

Previous week: one yellow after Thursday's RDL session, same pattern. Sleep averaged 7.2, stress 2 to 3, bodyweight flat.

Answering the agenda: no bad verdicts, no regression in force, budget met exactly, no 30-day exceedance, preparedness unremarkable, no below-glute. Question 1 and 2 are clean. But three yellows in two weeks on the RDL session is the drift the review exists to catch. Decision: the RDL is treated as regressed one rung for the coming week; range to mid-shin at 70 kg. The budget stays at 8. Nothing else changes. Had the tree been the only mechanism, Lena would have held at 70 kg from the floor indefinitely, each morning slightly stiff, each verdict a hold.

## A block review worked

Dev, end of his second Phase 2 block, 5 weeks, budget 10. From his weekly reviews: 5 clean weeks; one yellow in week 3; no cuts; no regressions. Trap-bar deadlift climbed from 140 kg × 5 to 150 kg × 5 at RPE 8; squat from 110 × 5 to 117.5 × 5. McGill retest: flexor/extensor ratio 0.85, side bridge/extensor 0.74, left/right within 3 percent, all held from the last retest. Bodyweight 84 kg.

Gates, read against the plan: floor deadlift at 1.5× bodyweight for 5 at RPE ≤ 8 with no yellow verdict needs 126 kg; the log says 150 from 2-inch blocks, not from the floor, so the gate is not met and the block-progression rule applies: he has had two clean weeks on 2-inch blocks and steps to the floor next block. Squat 1.5× bodyweight for 5 needs 126 kg; 117.5 is not there. Eight consecutive clean weeks: he has 5 in this block and 2 before it, 7. Ratios held.

Decisions: no phase advance; budget raised from 10 to 11, because the block had no cut and the lifts progressed; deload this week per the plan's rule, volume down 50 percent and intensity held; next block the deadlift comes to the floor, and the week it does, the daily tree treats the first floor session's verdict with the regression ladder ready. Everything in that paragraph was read from a table; none of it was decided on a day Dev felt strong or sore.

## Key points

<KeyList>

1. **The tree makes sessions smaller or holds them; the reviews are the only place things get bigger.** Budgets, phases and un-regressions are review decisions.
2. **Weekly: seven questions, from the log.** Verdicts, regressions, budget, 30-day maximum, sleep and stress, bodyweight, below-glute.
3. **The weekly review restores cuts, un-regresses one rung at a time, and catches drift.** Three yellows on one movement in two weeks is a regression the tree cannot see.
4. **Block: six questions, at the deload.** Clean weeks, gates by the numbers, ratios held, progression or stall, time spent regressed, number of cuts <Source id="bell-2023-deload-delphi" />.
5. **The budget rises by a pre-set step only after a block with no cut and real progression.** The dose-response is why it rises and why it rises slowly <Source id="schoenfeld-volume" />.
6. **Phases advance on the log's numbers, never on feel.** A gate is met when a logged set meets it.

</KeyList>

Sofia's weekly review: Phase 1, budget 8, baseline stiffness 3. Monday verdict on Sunday's session bad; the tree regressed her suitcase carry to a shorter distance. Thursday verdict on Wednesday's session bad again; the carry dropped to a lighter bell and the week's hard sets were cut 40 percent. Friday and Saturday clean. Sleep averaged 5.8 hours. What does the weekly review decide?
<Fragment slot="answer">
The budget cut carries into next week, because the week did not end without a further bad verdict after the second cut was applied, so the next week opens at 8 × 0.6, rounded to 5 hard sets, and reopens to 8 only after a full week with no bad verdict. The carry stays at its regressed rung, lighter and shorter, since it has not yet had two clean verdicts following its own sessions; it climbs back one rung at a time starting with load. The sleep average is noted as a preparedness pattern for the block review and caps the coming week's RPE ceiling. Why it matters: the weekly review is where cuts and regressions are carried or lifted, and it does so from counts, not from how the weekend felt.

<Check id="weekly-and-block-reviews-2" kind="apply">
Kenji's block review, end of a 6-week Phase 2 block, budget 12, bodyweight 90 kg. Weekly reviews show two cuts, in weeks 2 and 5. The log has a floor deadlift of 135 kg × 5 at RPE 8 with a clean verdict and squat at 140 kg × 5. Six clean weeks are not consecutive. Ratios held at retest. What does the block review decide about the budget and the phase?
<Fragment slot="answer">
The budget comes down, because two cuts in one block mean 12 hard sets was above his tolerance; he sets the next block at 11 or 10 per his pre-set step. The phase does not advance: the deadlift gate is 1.5 × 90 = 135 kg for 5 at RPE ≤ 8, which the log meets, and the squat gate of 135 kg is met at 140, but eight consecutive clean weeks is not, so the exit remains closed regardless of the lifts. The deload runs this week, and the next block is written at the lower budget with the same lifts. Why it matters: strong numbers do not override a block that cut twice, and every gate must read met from the log, not most of them.
</Fragment>
</Check>

<Check id="weekly-and-block-reviews-3" kind="concept">
In your own words, explain why a budget may be raised only at a block review and never by the daily tree or the weekly review.
<Fragment slot="answer">
The budget is the guard against gradual overreach, and gradual overreach is invisible on any single day and barely visible in a single week. A block is the shortest period over which the log can show both that the volume was tolerated, no cuts, and that it did something, the lifts progressed. Raising it on a good morning would let mood set the dose; raising it weekly would let one good week erase the information in the block. Why it matters: the guard only works if the thing it guards moves on a slower clock than the thing it is guarding against.
</Fragment>
</Check>
