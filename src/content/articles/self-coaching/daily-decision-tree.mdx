<Lede>Each morning the log yields a verdict on yesterday, and the verdict decides today. This article teaches the tree as a worked example first, then as a faded version for you to complete. The regression rule is the plan's own, a bad next-day verdict drops one rung on that movement, range then moment arm then load, never the movement's identity; two bad days in a week cut that week's axial hard sets by 40 percent.</Lede>

## The verdict

A verdict is a judgement about yesterday's session, read from this morning's [log](/learn/self-coaching/the-log/). It has three values.

| Verdict | Reading | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean** | Stiffness at or near baseline; below-glute no | Yesterday's session was tolerated |
| **Yellow** | Stiffness clearly above baseline, settles with the walk; below-glute no | Yesterday's session was at the edge |
| **Bad** | Stiffness well above baseline and does not settle, **or** below-glute yes | Yesterday's session exceeded tolerance |

"Clearly above baseline" is your call against your own fourteen-day run; a `[heuristic]` that has served is two points on a 0–10 scale. The morning test is the reading that counts because the disc's early-morning state makes stiffness most legible then <Source id="adams-dolan" />. A below-glute yes is a bad verdict on its own and also a trip to the [red-flag list](/learn/pathology/red-flags/) before anything else.

Sleep and stress do not change the verdict. They are preparedness: they decide how hard today may be, not what yesterday meant.

## The tree

Each morning, in order:

1. **Below-glute yes?** Check the red flags. If any apply, self-direction ends today. If none, the verdict is bad; continue.
2. **Verdict on yesterday.** Clean, yellow or bad, from the stiffness field.
3. **Bad → regress that movement one rung.** Range first, then moment arm, then load. Never the movement's identity. A hinge stays a hinge.
4. **Second bad day this week → cut this week's remaining axial hard sets by 40 percent.** Intensity held.
5. **Yellow → hold.** No progression on that movement today; no regression either.
6. **Clean → the spike guard decides progression.** One step, if the [trailing-30-day maximum](/learn/training-science/workload-spikes-and-acwr/) and the weekly budget allow it.
7. **Preparedness adjusts the ceiling.** Short sleep or a stress spike caps today's RPE a point lower <Source id="helms-rpe-rir" />. It never adds.

The whole rule set is `[heuristic]`, and the [plan](/plan/) tags it that way. Its logic is the plan's logic throughout: programming keys on range, moment arm, load and preparedness relative to your own history, never on "this movement is bad".

## The regression ladder

One rung down means the smallest change that keeps the movement. The order is fixed.

| Rung | What changes | Example, RDL |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Range | The movement gets shorter | Stop at mid-shin instead of below the knee |
| 2. Moment arm | The load moves closer | Dumbbells at the sides instead of a bar in front; or blocks |
| 3. Load | The bar gets lighter | Drop 10 percent, keep the sets |

Why that order: range and moment arm change the tissue demand at the same load, so they let a lifter keep the work and the practice while removing the part that exceeded tolerance <Source id="adams-dolan" />. Load comes last because it is the dial that the [progression model](/learn/training-science/progression-models/) is trying to move up. Movement identity is never a rung because swapping the hinge for a machine teaches the body nothing about hinging and teaches the lifter that the hinge is dangerous.

## Worked example

Tomas is in Phase 1. His baseline stiffness is 3 on his forward-reach test. His axial budget is 8 hard sets a week over two days, Tuesday and Friday. Tuesday he did RDLs from the floor at 90 kg, 3 × 10 at RPE 7, and trap-bar pulls at 100 kg, 3 × 5 at RPE 7; 6 axial hard sets.

**Wednesday morning.** Stiffness 6, does not settle with the walk, below-glute no. Sleep 7.5, stress 2.

Running the tree:

1. Below-glute is no; no red-flag check needed.
2. Stiffness is 3 above baseline and does not settle. **Verdict: bad.**
3. Regress. Which movement? Two were done. The rule regresses "that movement"; with two candidates the lifter regresses the one whose range or load changed most recently. Tomas added 5 kg to the RDL on Tuesday and the trap bar was unchanged. **RDL drops one rung: range.** Friday's RDL stops at mid-shin at 90 kg.
4. First bad day this week. No weekly cut.
5. Not yellow.
6. Not clean; no progression on anything Friday.
7. Preparedness fine; the RPE ceiling stays at 7.

**Friday's session:** RDL to mid-shin, 90 kg, 3 × 10 at RPE ≤ 7. Trap bar 100 kg, 3 × 5, unchanged. 2 hard sets remain in the budget and Tomas does not use them.

**Saturday morning.** Stiffness 4, settles with the walk. **Verdict: yellow.** Next week's RDL holds at mid-shin; nothing progresses on it until a clean verdict follows an RDL session.

Suppose instead Saturday had read stiffness 6, not settling. Second bad day in the week. The RDL drops another rung, moment arm, to dumbbells at the sides at a matched load; and the weekly 40 percent cut applies. His budget was 8; he had used 6; the cut is on the week's hard sets, so the following week opens with a budget of 8 × 0.6, rounded to 5, until the [weekly review](/learn/self-coaching/weekly-and-block-reviews/) restores it. Intensity holds at RPE 7; it is sets that come off, because volume is the dial that accumulates <Source id="schoenfeld-volume" />.

## Key points

<KeyList>

1. **The verdict is about yesterday, read this morning.** Clean, yellow or bad, from the stiffness field and the below-glute yes/no.
2. **Bad → one rung down on that movement.** Range, then moment arm, then load. Never the movement's identity.
3. **Two bad days in a week → cut that week's axial hard sets by 40 percent.** Intensity held; sets come off <Source id="schoenfeld-volume" />.
4. **Yellow holds; clean lets the spike guard decide.** Progression is never a reward for feeling good; it is a step the 30-day maximum and the budget allow <Source id="impellizzeri-2020-acwr" />.
5. **Sleep and stress cap today's RPE; they never raise it.** Preparedness adjusts the ceiling, not the verdict <Source id="helms-rpe-rir" />.
6. **A below-glute yes is a bad verdict and a red-flag check, in that order.** The tree does not run until the list has been read.

</KeyList>

<Check kind="concept">
In your own words, explain why movement identity is never a rung on the regression ladder.
<Fragment slot="answer">
The ladder's job is to find the smallest change that brings yesterday's session inside tolerance while keeping the lifter practising the movement they are building. Range and moment arm reduce the demand at the same load; load reduces it while keeping the pattern. Swapping the movement for a different one removes the practice entirely and teaches that the pattern itself was the problem, which is the belief the whole site argues against: tolerance is relative to history, not to a movement. Why it matters: a lifter who abandons the hinge after a bad day has learned fear, not programming.
</Fragment>
</Check>
