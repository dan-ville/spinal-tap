<Lede>A block is four to six weeks of training written down before the first session, so that nothing in it is decided on a day you feel strong or sore. This article names the seven decisions a block needs, works a complete Phase 1 block, then hands you the same block with the last two decisions blank, and finally a scenario you write from scratch.</Lede>

## Why the block is written before it starts

The [log](/learn/self-coaching/the-log/) records, the [daily tree](/learn/self-coaching/daily-decision-tree/) shrinks or holds a session, and the [reviews](/learn/self-coaching/weekly-and-block-reviews/) move budgets and phases. None of them can write a session. That is the block's job, and it has to be done once, in advance, because every rule on this site reads a decision that already exists: the tree regresses "one rung" on a ladder that must already be named, the weekly review asks whether "the budget" was met, the spike guard compares today against a maximum that was planned. A lifter who writes Tuesday's session on Tuesday has nothing for those rules to read.

The evidence for planning the variation at all is thin but points one way: periodized plans beat non-periodized ones for maximal strength in the pooled trials, with low certainty and short trials <Source id="williams-2017-periodization-meta" />. The [periodization article](/learn/training-science/periodization/) covers the calendar shapes; a first block is the simplest of them, a short accumulation with a deload at the end.

## The seven decisions

Every block answers these, in this order, on one page.

| # | Decision | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Phase and length** | Which plan phase, how many loading weeks, which week is the deload |
| 2 | **Lifts** | One hinge, one squat, one pull, one carry; named with their starting range |
| 3 | **Weekly axial hard-set budget** | The cap on sets at RPE ≥ 7 across the main lifts, and which days hold them |
| 4 | **Rep ranges and RPE cap** | Per lift, the range; for the block, the phase's cap <Source id="helms-rpe-rir" /> |
| 5 | **Progression rule per lift** | Double progression or APRE, with the load step written as a number |
| 6 | **Regression ladder per lift** | The three rungs, range then moment arm then load, named for each lift before any verdict needs them |
| 7 | **Dates** | Weekly review day, block review date, deload week, any retest |

Decisions 1 to 4 set the size of the block; 5 and 6 set how it moves in each direction; 7 sets when it is read. The sixth decision is the one self-coached lifters skip, and it is the one the tree cannot run without: "drop one rung on that movement" is an instruction only if the rungs exist on paper.

Two conventions, both `[heuristic]`. The budget counts the hinge, squat and pull; carries sit outside it and progress on the [strongman ladder](/learn/training-science/strongman-loading/), distance first, then load. And the first week's loads are whatever reads RPE 6 at the bottom of the rep range, so that the block starts with room under the cap.

## A complete block, worked

Priya has finished Phase 0 with ratios inside the gates and a fourteen-day baseline stiffness of 2. Bodyweight 62 kg. She has never followed a written block.

| # | Decision | Priya's block |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phase and length | Phase 1. Four loading weeks, deload in week 5, block review at the end of week 5 |
| 2 | Lifts | Hinge: Romanian deadlift from the floor. Squat: front squat to full depth. Pull: trap-bar deadlift from 4-inch blocks. Carry: farmer's walk and suitcase carry |
| 3 | Budget | 8 axial hard sets per week. Tuesday: RDL 3 + trap bar 2 = 5. Friday: front squat 3 = 3. Carries both days, outside the budget |
| 4 | Ranges and cap | RDL 3 × 8 to 12. Front squat 3 × 6 to 8. Trap bar 2 × 5. RPE cap 7, as Phase 1 requires |
| 5 | Progression | Double progression on all three. RDL +2.5 kg when all sets reach 12. Front squat +2.5 kg when all sets reach 8. Trap bar +5 kg when both sets of 5 read RPE ≤ 7 for two consecutive sessions. Carries: +10 m per week to 40 m, then +4 kg per hand and back to 20 m |
| 6 | Regression ladders | RDL: range → stop at mid-shin; moment arm → dumbbells held at the sides; load → −10%. Front squat: range → box set just above depth; moment arm → goblet hold; load → −10%. Trap bar: range → 6-inch blocks; moment arm → high handles; load → −10%. Carries: distance → −10 m; load → −4 kg per hand |
| 7 | Dates | Weekly review every Sunday. Deload week 5, volume down 50%, intensity held. Block review Sunday of week 5, with the four McGill holds retested that day |

Starting loads, from the RPE 6 convention: RDL 50 kg, front squat 40 kg, trap bar 70 kg, farmer's 16 kg per hand for 20 m.

Read the table against the rules it serves. If Wednesday morning in week 2 reads bad, the tree does not have to think: it finds "RDL: range → mid-shin" and Friday's RDL is written. If a second bad day lands that week, the weekly cut takes 8 to 5 hard sets, which the Sunday review carries or lifts. If a clean verdict follows a session where all RDL sets hit 12, the step is 2.5 kg and not a number chosen on the day. The budget is the reason the block has only 8 sets in it despite a dose-response that would reward more: the dose-response is real, and so are its diminishing returns and the plan's need for a cap that can be cut <Source id="schoenfeld-volume" />. And week 5 is a deload, not a regression: volume halves and the loads stay, the form the practitioner consensus recommends <Source id="bell-2023-deload-delphi" />.

## The same block, last two decisions blank

Omar, Phase 1, baseline stiffness 3, bodyweight 88 kg. His first five decisions are made. Write the sixth and seventh before opening the Check.

| # | Decision | Omar's block |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phase and length | Phase 1. Five loading weeks, deload in week 6, block review at the end of week 6 |
| 2 | Lifts | Hinge: RDL from the floor. Squat: safety-bar squat to full depth. Pull: trap-bar deadlift from 2-inch blocks. Carry: suitcase carry |
| 3 | Budget | 9 axial hard sets per week. Monday: RDL 3 + safety-bar squat 2 = 5. Thursday: trap bar 2 + safety-bar squat 2 = 4 |
| 4 | Ranges and cap | RDL 3 × 8 to 12. Safety-bar squat 2 × 6 to 8 on both days. Trap bar 2 × 5. RPE cap 7 |
| 5 | Progression | Double progression. RDL +2.5 kg at 3 × 12. Squat +2.5 kg at 8, 8 on both days in the same week. Trap bar +5 kg after two consecutive sessions at RPE ≤ 7. Suitcase carry +10 m per week to 40 m, then +4 kg |
| 6 | Regression ladders | ? |
| 7 | Dates | ? |

Omar's ladders will not be Priya's, because the squat starts under a different bar and the pull starts from lower blocks. The rungs are still range, then moment arm, then load, and the lift keeps its name on every rung.

## A block from scratch

Nadia is entering Phase 2. Bodyweight 70 kg, baseline stiffness 2. Her last Phase 1 block ran at a budget of 8 with no cuts and the block review raised it to 10. Her log shows: trap-bar deadlift from 4-inch blocks 95 kg × 5 at RPE 7; front squat 60 kg × 8 at RPE 7; RDL 72.5 kg × 12 at RPE 7; farmer's walk 24 kg per hand for 40 m. Her trailing-30-day single-session maxima are those same numbers. Phase 2 lets the pull step down one block height per two clean weeks, allows RPE 8, and wants a deload every four to six weeks.

Write all seven decisions. Then, for one lift, show the first two weeks' sessions as they would appear in the log, with the decision column empty, so that the daily tree has something to read. The model answer is in the second Check; yours will differ in the numbers and should not differ in the structure.

## Key points

<KeyList>

1. **A block is seven decisions written before the first session.** Phase and length, lifts, budget, ranges and cap, progression rule, regression ladders, dates.
2. **The regression ladders are written per lift, in advance.** Range, then moment arm, then load, each named as a specific variant; the tree reads them, it does not invent them.
3. **The budget is the block's size, and it only moves at the block review.** The dose-response rewards more sets and the cap exists so that volume can be cut when verdicts say so <Source id="schoenfeld-volume" />.
4. **Load steps are numbers, not feelings.** Each progression rule names its condition and its step, and the spike guard checks the step against the trailing 30 days <Source id="impellizzeri-2020-acwr" />.
5. **The deload is scheduled when the block is written.** Volume down, intensity held, on a date, not when a bad week forces it <Source id="bell-2023-deload-delphi" />.
6. **The RPE cap belongs to the phase, not the lifter's mood.** Preparedness can lower it for a day; nothing raises it inside a block <Source id="helms-rpe-rir" />.

</KeyList>

<Check id="writing-your-first-block-1" kind="apply">
Write Omar's sixth and seventh decisions.
<Fragment slot="answer">
Six, the ladders. RDL: range → mid-shin; moment arm → dumbbells at the sides; load → −10%. Safety-bar squat: range → box just above depth; moment arm → goblet or front-rack hold with the same depth; load → −10%. Trap bar from 2-inch blocks: range → 4-inch blocks; moment arm → high handles; load → −10%. Suitcase carry: distance → −10 m; load → −4 kg. Every rung keeps the lift's name. Seven, the dates. Weekly review on a fixed day, say Sunday. Deload in week 6 with volume down 50% and intensity held. Block review on the Sunday of week 6, McGill holds retested that day, and the Phase 1 gate "4 consecutive weeks without a bad verdict" read from the five weekly reviews. Why it matters: these two decisions are the ones the tree and the reviews read directly; without them a bad morning has no rung to drop to and a block has no end.

<Check id="writing-your-first-block-3" kind="concept">
In your own words, explain why the regression ladders must be written before the block starts rather than chosen on the morning of a bad verdict.
<Fragment slot="answer">
A bad morning is the worst time to design anything: the lifter is stiff, worried, and tempted either to drop the movement entirely or to push through it. A ladder written in advance turns that morning into a lookup. It also guarantees that the rung keeps the lift's identity, because the ladder was written in the plan's order, range then moment arm then load, when no one was frightened of the hinge. Why it matters: the tree's instruction is "one rung down", and an instruction needs a ladder that already exists.
</Fragment>
</Check>
