<Lede>Every rule in the plan reads from a log, and the log is five daily fields that take under two minutes. This article specifies the fields exactly as Phase 0 defines them, shows what a filled week looks like, explains what each field is for, and sets out the two habits that keep a log honest, recording on bad days and never editing yesterday.</Lede>

## Why a log before a programme

A self-coached lifter has no one watching. A coach notices that you walked in stiff, that you slept badly, that the last two Mondays were worse than the Thursdays. The log is that coach's memory, written by you, read by the rules. The [plan](/plan/) opens with two weeks of logging and no change to training at all, and its first exit gate is fourteen consecutive check-ins, because every later rule, the regression ladder, the weekly cut, the spike guard, is a function of entries that do not exist until you write them.

The [cognitive functional therapy trial](/learn/rehab/cognitive-functional-therapy/) that produced the largest durable effect in chronic back pain so far made sleep, stress and activity explicit targets rather than background noise <Source id="kent-2023-restore" />. The log is where those become numbers you can look at.

## The five daily fields

These are the Phase 0 check-in fields from the [plan](/plan/), recorded every day, training day or not:

| Field | Format | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Morning stiffness test | Your own score, 0–10 | The same test, same time, same way, every day |
| Anything felt below the glute | yes / no | Below the gluteal fold, either leg, at any point in the day |
| Hours slept | number, to the half hour | Time asleep, not time in bed |
| Life stress | 1–5 | Your own scale; consistency matters more than the anchors |
| Bodyweight | kg, one decimal | Same scale, same time, same state |

Two of these need a word.

**The stiffness test is identical or it is useless.** Pick one movement, standing forward reach to a mark, or a seated slump to a fixed point, or how many degrees the hips turn in a fixed-feet rotation, and do it at the same time each morning, the same number of repetitions, before coffee and before the walk. Score it against yourself. The disc is at its most hydrated and its stiffest in flexion in the first hour after waking <Source id="adams-dolan" />, which is why the test lives in the morning and why its timing cannot drift: a test at 7 am and the same test at 10 am measure different spines. The number is only meaningful as a change from your own baseline. The point is not what you can reach today; it is whether today is different from the fourteen days that preceded it.

**Below the glute is a yes/no, and yes is a routing decision.** Anything felt below the gluteal fold, ache, tingling, a line of heat, a dead patch, is recorded as yes, with a one-word note on where. A single yes is a data point; the [pathology track's red-flag article](/learn/pathology/red-flags/) sets out which patterns end self-direction and go to a clinician, and the plan repeats them. The field is binary on purpose. Grading leg symptoms invites negotiation with yourself on a day you would rather train.

## Training-day fields

On a lifting day, the log also takes, per lift, the load, sets, reps and RPE of the working sets <Source id="helms-rpe-rir" />. Those four columns are the input to the [volume article's](/learn/training-science/volume-intensity-frequency/) hard-set count and the [spike guard's](/learn/training-science/workload-spikes-and-acwr/) trailing-30-day maximum. Monthly, the McGill battery times and ratios go in as well <Source id="mcgill-1999-endurance-norms" />; the [plan's calculator](/plan/) computes them.

## A filled week

A lifter two weeks into Phase 1. Stiffness is a forward reach to a mark scored 0 (reaches easily) to 10 (cannot bend).

| Day | Stiffness | Below glute | Sleep | Stress | Bodyweight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 3 | no | 7.5 | 2 | 86.2 |
| Tue | 3 | no | 7.0 | 2 | 86.0 |
| Wed | 5 | no | 5.5 | 4 | 86.1 |
| Thu | 4 | no | 7.0 | 3 | 85.9 |
| Fri | 3 | no | 8.0 | 2 | 86.0 |
| Sat | 3 | no | 8.0 | 1 | 86.3 |
| Sun | 2 | no | 8.5 | 1 | 86.1 |

Wednesday is the only day that stands out, and it stands out on three fields at once: a stiffer morning after a short night with a stress spike. Tuesday was a lifting day. The [daily decision tree](/learn/self-coaching/daily-decision-tree/) is where Wednesday's morning becomes a verdict on Tuesday's session, and that verdict is only possible because the other six days establish what normal looks like.

## Two habits that keep it honest

**Record on bad days.** A log with gaps on the days that hurt is a log of the days that did not, and it will tell you that you are fine. The fourteen-consecutive-days exit gate exists to make this a habit before the stakes rise.

**Never edit yesterday.** A stiffness score of 6 that you later decide was "really a 4 once I warmed up" has been replaced by a memory, and memories of pain are reconstructed. The log records what the morning test said at the time it was done. If you want to add context, add a note; do not change the number.

## What the log is not

It is not a pain diary. A pain diary records symptoms; this records five inputs, one of which is a stiffness test and one of which is a yes/no, and the rest of which are about sleep, stress and mass. The [pain-science track](/learn/pain-science/pain-vs-damage/) explains why a day-by-day symptom score tends to pull attention toward the symptom; the log is built to pull attention toward preparedness instead.

It is also not a mood score, a streak, or a performance dashboard. The site refuses streak metrics everywhere, and the log is the first place that matters: the exit gate is fourteen consecutive entries, and after that the log's value lies in its content, not its length.

## Key points

<KeyList>

1. **Five fields, every day, training or not.** An identical morning stiffness test, anything below the glute (yes/no), hours slept, life stress, bodyweight. They are the Phase 0 check-in in the [plan](/plan/).
2. **The stiffness test is identical or it is useless.** Same movement, same time of morning, scored against your own baseline. The disc's morning state is why timing cannot drift <Source id="adams-dolan" />.
3. **Below the glute is binary.** A yes routes to the red-flag list; it is not graded.
4. **Training days add load, sets, reps and RPE per lift.** These feed the hard-set count and the 30-day spike guard <Source id="helms-rpe-rir" />.
5. **Record on bad days and never edit yesterday.** A log of good days says you are fine; an edited number is a memory.
6. **The log is the coach's memory.** Every later rule reads from it, which is why Phase 0 is two weeks of logging and nothing else.

</KeyList>

<Check id="the-log-1" kind="recall">
List the five daily check-in fields and the format of each.
<Fragment slot="answer">
A morning stiffness test performed identically each day, scored against your own baseline; anything felt below the glute, yes or no; hours slept, as time asleep; life stress on a short personal scale; bodyweight in kg under the same conditions. Why it matters: the rules in the plan read these exact fields, and a log with a different set of columns cannot drive them.

<Check id="the-log-2" kind="apply">
A lifter named Priya does her stiffness test as a seated slump to a mark. On Monday she tests at 6:45 am and scores 2. On Tuesday she oversleeps, tests at 10:15 am after a walk, and scores 1. Is Tuesday's score usable, and what should she record?
<Fragment slot="answer">
Tuesday's number is not comparable to Monday's. The test was done three and a half hours later and after a walk, when the disc has lost its early-morning hydration and stiffness, so a lower score is expected regardless of how her back is. She should record the 1 with a note that the test was late and post-walk, never alter it, and treat the day as missing for the purpose of a verdict. Why it matters: the stiffness field only means something as a change from baseline, and the baseline is defined by the identical conditions.
</Fragment>
</Check>

<Check id="the-log-3" kind="concept">
In your own words, explain why the log is a yes/no on leg symptoms rather than a 0–10 score, when the stiffness test is a score.
<Fragment slot="answer">
The stiffness test tracks a change in your own baseline, and a scale is the right tool for a change. Leg symptoms are a routing question: their presence, not their size, is what the red-flag list keys on, and a scale would invite you to argue that a 2 does not count on a day you want to train. A binary field removes the negotiation. Why it matters: a log field's format should match the decision it feeds.
</Fragment>
</Check>
