# Rehab-to-performance plan

<Lede>A five-phase route from training around a back that limits you to competing on it. You move on when your own records say you are ready, never when a calendar does.</Lede>

<h2 id="rules">The five rules underneath</h2>

These run through every phase. Each has a one-word handle so it sticks.

<ol class="rules">
  <li><span class="rule-title">Log</span><p>Five lines every morning: how stiff your back feels, anything felt below the glute, sleep, stress, bodyweight. The <Term id="daily-check-in">log</Term>, not how you feel in the gym, drives every decision on this page.</p></li>
  <li><span class="rule-title">Verdict</span><p>Each morning gives yesterday a <Term id="verdict">verdict</Term>: clean, yellow or bad. Clean lets you progress, yellow holds, bad makes one movement a little easier in a fixed order. Two bad days in one week make the rest of the week lighter.</p></li>
  <li><span class="rule-title">Gates</span><p>You leave a phase through its <Term id="exit-gate">exit gates</Term>: measurements you tick off in your own tracker, such as a hold time or a lift at a multiple of your bodyweight. There are no dates.</p></li>
  <li><span class="rule-title">Starting numbers</span><p>Every number here is a <Term id="starting-number">starting number</Term>. As your records fill, your own measurements replace it, and the page says so wherever it matters.</p></li>
  <li><span class="rule-title">Red flags</span><p><Term id="red-flags">Red flags</Term> end self-direction. The symptoms in the red box below send you to a clinician the same day, in any phase.</p></li>
</ol>

<h2 id="phase-0">Phase 0 — Baseline</h2>

<p class="phase-meta">About 2 weeks · 1 exit gate</p>

<p class="plain"><strong>In plain words.</strong> For two weeks nothing changes in your training. You start writing things down every morning, measure where your trunk endurance stands, and film your main lifts. The point is to turn guesses into a log before any rule has to read from it.</p>

### What you do

- A <Term id="red-flags">red-flag screen</Term>, written down. Repeat it every month.
- The <Term id="daily-check-in">daily check-in</Term>, training day or not: the <Term id="morning-stiffness-test">morning stiffness test</Term> done the same way each day, anything felt <Term id="below-the-glute">below the glute</Term> (yes or no), hours slept, life stress, bodyweight.
- The <Term id="mcgill-battery">McGill endurance battery</Term>, once: the <Term id="flexor-hold">trunk flexor hold</Term>, the <Term id="biering-sorensen">Biering-Sørensen extensor hold</Term>, and a <Term id="side-bridge">side bridge</Term> on each side. Write down the seconds. <Tag source="mcgill-1999-endurance-norms" />
- <Term id="filming-lifts">Film</Term> your squat, deadlift and RDL at working weights. You will re-film and compare each month.
- Minutes walked each day.

The four holds give three <Term id="endurance-ratios">endurance ratios</Term>: flexor against extensor, side bridge against extensor, and left side against right. The same three ratios are gates in Phase 1 and Phase 2. The <a href="/templates/">tracker spreadsheet</a> works them out from your hold times.

<h2 id="phase-1">Phase 1 — Capacity floor</h2>

<p class="phase-meta">4–8 weeks, typically · 6 exit gates</p>

<p class="plain"><strong>In plain words.</strong> You build a floor: daily walking and short trunk holds, two lifting days a week that stay well short of all-out, and some carrying. Nothing is heavy yet. The floor is ready when your trunk holds are balanced, you have gone a month without a bad morning, and you can hinge your own bodyweight and carry half of it in one hand.</p>

### What you do

- **Every day.** Walk 20–30 minutes. <Tag kind="proposed" /> The <Term id="mcgill-big-3">McGill big 3</Term> as endurance work, in a descending pyramid (for example 5, 3 and 1 repetitions of 10-second holds), done at least an hour after waking <Tag kind="heuristic" /> because the disc is at its stiffest early in the day.
- **Two lifting days a week, not back to back.** Main lifts at <Term id="rpe">RPE 7</Term> or easier, meaning three reps left in the tank, with <Term id="grinding">no grinding</Term>. <Tag kind="proposed" /> <Tag source="helms-rpe-rir" /> Squat at around <Term id="percentage">65–75% of your estimated max</Term>. <Tag kind="proposed" /> Pull from <Term id="pulls-from-blocks">blocks</Term> or with a <Term id="trap-bar">trap bar</Term>. The <Term id="rdl">RDL</Term> is the main hinge builder, progressed by <Term id="double-progression">double progression</Term> from 3×8 up to 3×12. <Tag kind="proposed" />
- **Twice a week.** <Term id="loaded-carries">Loaded carries</Term> (farmer's and suitcase) and <Term id="anti-rotation">anti-rotation work</Term>. <Tag kind="proposed" />

### When a morning is bad

The <Term id="regression-rule">regression rule</Term> <Tag kind="heuristic" /> is the plan's answer to a bad <Term id="verdict">verdict</Term>, and it runs the same way in every phase. The movement that most recently changed gets one step easier, and the steps come in a fixed order:

<div class="ladder">

1. **Range.** The movement gets shorter. An RDL stops at mid-shin instead of below the knee.
2. **Moment arm.** The weight moves closer to you. Dumbbells at your sides instead of a bar in front, or a pull from blocks.
3. **Load.** The bar gets lighter. The sets stay.

</div>

**It is never the movement itself.** A hinge stays a hinge; it gets shorter, closer or lighter. And if a second bad morning lands in the same week, that week's remaining <Term id="hard-set">hard sets</Term> on <Term id="axial-loading">spine-loading lifts</Term> are cut by 40 percent. The weight stays; the number of hard sets comes down. The [daily decision tree](/learn/self-coaching/daily-decision-tree/) walks through a week of this with worked examples.

<h2 id="phase-2">Phase 2 — Progressive loading</h2>

<p class="phase-meta">8–16 weeks, typically · 4 exit gates</p>

<p class="plain"><strong>In plain words.</strong> The weights start going up on purpose. The deadlift comes back to the floor in steps, every week has a fixed number of hard sets you may spend on the spine, and every month or so you take a planned easier week. You leave when squat and deadlift reach one and a half times your bodyweight for five, after two clean months, with your trunk holds still balanced.</p>

### What you do

- **The deadlift returns to the floor by steps.** <Term id="pulls-from-blocks">Blocks</Term> at 4 inches, then 2 inches, then the floor: one step down after every two <Term id="clean-week">clean weeks</Term>. <Tag kind="proposed" />
- **Progress by <Term id="double-progression">double progression</Term> or <Term id="apre">APRE</Term>.** <Term id="deload">Deload</Term> every 4–6 weeks: sets down 40–60 percent, weight held. <Tag kind="proposed" /> <Tag source="schoenfeld-volume" />
- **Strength gates are read from an estimate, never a tested max.** The bodyweight multiples below come off an <Term id="e1rm">estimated one-rep max</Term> from a recent <Term id="working-load">working set</Term>. <Tag kind="proposed" /> The tracker computes it on every logged set.
- **A weekly <Term id="hard-set-budget">hard-set budget</Term> for spine-loading lifts.** You spend it inside the week and do not borrow from the next. The <Term id="spike-guard">spike guard</Term> is your biggest single session in the trailing 30 days: today may be one step above it, not a jump. The plan does not use the <Term id="acwr">acute:chronic workload ratio</Term>. <Tag kind="contested" />

<h2 id="phase-3">Phase 3 — Strength build</h2>

<p class="phase-meta">6–12 months, typically · 2 exit gates</p>

<p class="plain"><strong>In plain words.</strong> Now it is a strength programme, organised in blocks of a month or so with one job each. Strongman implements come in through their easier versions first. The rules from Phase 2 keep running underneath. You leave when you have competed once and strung three blocks together without injury.</p>

### What you do

- **<Term id="block-periodization">Block periodization</Term>.** Accumulation, then intensification, then realization, each block 4–6 weeks. <Tag kind="proposed" /> <Tag source="schoenfeld-volume" /> Deloads and the hard-set budget carry over from Phase 2 unchanged.
- **<Term id="strongman-implements">Strongman implements</Term> enter as their easier version first** <Tag kind="heuristic" />: a yoke walk before a yoke-and-turn, sandbag to lap before sandbag over bar, stone to a high platform before a low one. Each new implement starts at the bottom of the regression ladder and earns its range, moment arm and load in that order.
- **A distant marker.** A powerlifting <Term id="dots">DOTS score</Term> around 450 is roughly the top 1 percent of open competitive entries. <Tag kind="heuristic" /> It is a landmark years away, not a gate. The tracker computes DOTS from a total and bodyweight.

<h2 id="assessment">One thing a website cannot give you</h2>

**One <Term id="physio-assessment">physiotherapy assessment</Term> is the cheapest input** that can turn guesses about *your* back into findings.
